Design exploration

Primary nav — four ways to clear the clutter

Today the header shows six equally-weighted destinations. Four of them — Daily, Monthly, Reconcile and Search — are all things you do inside spending, yet they sit beside Home and Loans as if they were peers. Four concepts below rebalance that hierarchy without removing any capability.

Why the top bar feels cluttered

  • Six flat, equal items. Home · Daily · Monthly · Search · Reconcile · Loans — no hierarchy, so every label competes equally for attention.
  • Mixed kinds of thing. Home and Loans are places; Search and Reconcile are verbs / one-off tasks.
  • Duplicated grouping. Daily/Monthly/Reconcile/Search all live under /spending, but the bar hides that relationship.
  • Mobile turns into a 3×2 grid that eats a third of the viewport before any content shows.
  • Active state is subtle — one small dark pill among five grey ones; easy to lose your place.
  • No room to grow. A seventh destination forces a wrap or a redesign anyway.

The concepts

Recommended

1 · Grouped two-tier

Top level collapses to Home · Spending · Loans. Inside Spending, a segmented sub-row (Daily · Monthly · Reconcile · Search) appears — the same pattern already used by Today / Plan / Goals.

Home Spending Loans

6 → 3 top-level items. Context appears only where it's needed. Search becomes an action, not a place.
Smallest change

2 · Four + “More”

Keep the four most-used destinations visible and tuck the rest (Reconcile · Search · Loans) behind a More ▾ menu. Least disruptive to muscle memory.

Home Daily Monthly More ▾

Trade-off: hides three destinations one click deep; items swap rank as usage changes.
Action-oriented

3 · Task-first with a CTA

Group by job-to-be-done: Track (Daily + Monthly), Find (search icon), with Reconcile promoted to a prominent primary button — the thing you actually must do each month.

Home Track Find Reconcile

6 → 4 elements, but introduces unfamiliar labels; needs onboarding copy.
Most room to grow

4 · Side rail + bottom tab bar

Move navigation into a vertical left rail on desktop and a bottom tab bar on mobile. The top bar becomes brand + account only.

☰ Overview

Biggest change, most scalable; keeps all six destinations plus room for more.

Recommendation

Ship Concept 1 (Grouped two-tier). It matches the spend pages' real structure, re-uses the segmentation pattern already on the Daily page, and drops the header from six competing labels to three. Keep Concept 2's “More” menu as a fallback if you want the smallest possible diff, and borrow Concept 3's idea of promoting Reconcile — the one genuinely recurring task — to a button inside the Spending sub-row.
